# Training, 2026-11-10 (after gen 14)

Gen 14 (masked judge: no card identities of our hand or board) is the benchmark. All gates: 200 mirror games vs
`learned:gen_014`, seeds 90000, 55% needed to promote.

| Candidate | Data | Score |
|---|---|---|
| cand_g15lin (linear) | gens 6-8 + 1000 cand_mhb games + 3000 gen-14 self-play games | 45.5% |
| cand_g15nn (wide-and-deep, 16 hidden) | same | 45.5% |
| cand_g15on (linear) | only the 3000 gen-14 games | 39.5% |
| cand_g15bd (linear) | gens 6-8 + turn-boundary rows of the 3000 gen-14 games (no what-if rows) | 46.0% |

Findings:
- A bigger model does not help: the 16-unit net learned nothing over the linear judge (val loss 0.5232 vs 0.5235)
  and played the same 91/200.
- More self-play data from gen 14 makes the judge worse, and the more of it, the worse (gen-14 data alone: 39.5%).
  It is not the what-if rows (dropping them: 46%). Gen 14 was trained on the games of older, card-aware AIs
  (gens 3-8); its own games seem to cover too narrow a set of positions to learn from.
- Next (running): mixed self-play, each seat picks one of gens 14 / 8 / 6 / the hand-written score, with twice the
  exploration (`train_loop selfplay --policy a,b,c --epsilon 0.06`), then the same masked linear training.
