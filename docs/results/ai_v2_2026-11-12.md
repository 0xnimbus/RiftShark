# AI v2, steps 1-2: policy net and two-turn search (2026-11-11/12)

Plan: docs/plans/ai_v2.md. Benchmark: gen 14 (`learned:gen_014`). Gates are mirror matches against gen 14, seed 90000
(a copy of gen 14 scores exactly 50% there).

## Step 1: policy net (a fast copy of gen 14)

- Data: 1500 gen-14 self-play games recorded with every option of every decision (`tools/policy_data.py`), 160,801
  decisions with two or more options.
- Model: `riftsim/core/learn/policy_net.py`, one hidden tanh layer (64) over the position features and 168 option
  features (kind, hashed card identity, cost, stats, destination). Trained by `tools/train_policy.py`.
- Agreement with gen 14 on held-out games: 61% (chance 30%); 68% on main-phase action decisions.
- Playing alone (`policy:policy_g14`) vs gen 14: **18%** (100 games). About 100x faster per move.

## Step 2: two-turn search with the policy net as the opponent model

`learned:gen_014:p=policy_g14[:psN][:b=X][:dN][:crn]`: gen 14's one-turn judgement ranks the options; the top 3
(plus end turn / pass) are played on determinised copies through the opponent's whole turn (opponent model inside,
our answers too) and judged at the start of our next turn.

| Variant | Opponent model in the search | Score vs gen 14 | Games |
|---|---|---|---|
| deep value decides | policy net | 27% (16/60, stopped) | 60 |
| blend 0.5 shallow + deep, 3 samples | policy net | 44% | 100 |
| deep value decides | gen 14 judging only the policy net's top 3 options (`ps3`) | 35% | 100 |
| same, common random numbers (`crn`) | `ps3` | 41.7% | 100 |
| same, common random numbers, blend 0.5 | `ps3` | 47.4% | 95 |
| same, common random numbers, blend 0.3, 6 samples | `ps3` | 51.4% (30 of 100 games over the 300 s limit, dropped) | 70 |
| same, game limit 2400 s (clean run) | `ps3` | 51.5% (interval ~42-61%) | 99 |
| same, **10 samples** (seed 91000, limit 4000 s) | `ps3` | **65.0%** (95% interval 55-74%) | 100 |
| confirmation on fresh seeds (93000) | `ps3` | **61.9%** (60/97, interval 52-71%; 3 games over 4000 s) | 97 |

## Why the deeper look loses: noise

A diagnostic (8 samples per candidate, same determinisations for every candidate) at gen-14 decisions: the value of
one candidate after the opponent's turn varies with the sample by **sd 70-115** (in 1000 x (win prob - 0.5) units), and
the difference between two candidates on the *same* samples still varies by sd 20-190. With 3 samples the error of a
difference is ~15-110, while the gaps between gen 14's top three options are usually 10-60. So the two-turn value
mostly picks at random among the top candidates, and throws away the one-turn ranking that is reliable (its own
noise is small because only the rest of our turn is simulated). Getting the error below the gaps needs ~30+ samples
per candidate, i.e. ~10x the cost of the current searching AI (already ~5x gen 14) in pure Python.

The opponent model was not the bottleneck: a stronger one (`ps3`, close to gen 14) did no better than the policy net.

## Result: promoted as `search-v2`

Two independent 100-game gates: 65.0% and 61.9% (together 125 of 197, 63.5%). `make_policy("search-v2")` is this
AI. Gen 14 stays the fast default (play page, self-play); search-v2 is the new benchmark for strength.

Lessons: the opponent model mattered less than the noise. Shared samples, blending with the one-turn ranking and
more samples each helped, monotonically: 35% -> 42% -> 47% -> 51.5% (6 samples) -> 63.5% (10 samples).

## Next: step 3 (expert iteration)

Search-v2 is too slow for large self-play (~20 games/h on 4 cores). Plan: record search-v2's choices and the game
results over a few hundred games, train the policy net on its choices and the judge on its positions, and gate
"gen 14 judge + new policy net in the same search" and "new judge alone" against gen 14 / search-v2.

## Step 3, first pass (2026-11-13)

- Recorded 200 search-v2 self-play games (22,189 decisions, `tools/policy_data.py --policy search-v2`; ~13 games/h on
  4 cores; two container restarts cost ~60 games, checkpoints are now every 10 games).
- Policy net retrained on gen-14 data + these games (x3 weight), `models/policy_sv2a.json`: as a stand-alone player it
  scored 48.5% against the old policy net (200 games): no measurable gain. On the search-v2 games alone it overfits
  (val agreement 49%). 22k decisions are too few to move it.
- Adaptive samples (`aN:gX`: 4 shared samples, two more while the best two are within 15, up to 10):
  `learned:gen_014:p=policy_g14:ps3:crn:b=0.3:d10:a4:g15` vs search-v2: **50.5%** (99 games), but it used 7.6 samples
  per deep decision instead of 10 and was only ~15% faster per move (the one-turn judgement of every option and the
  pruned opponent model cost as much as the extra samples).
- An engine speed attempt (memoising off-board triggered abilities in `Game._trigger_index`) gave identical games and no
  measurable speed-up; reverted. Profile of search-v2 play: `_trigger_index` 43% cumulative, `_compute_chars` 28%,
  cloning 10%: characteristics are recomputed after every state change on every determinised copy.

Conclusion: expert iteration needs far more search games than this machine produces (~300/day). The lever is engine
speed (caching characteristics across versions where nothing relevant changed) or more machines.
- 2026-11-14: 300 search-v2 games (32,536 decisions, `/mnt/project-files/riftshark/learn/pol_sv2.npz`); policy net
  retrained on gen-14 data + these (x3): 45% vs the old policy net as a stand-alone player (200 games). Distilling
  search-v2's choices into the policy net does not help at this data scale.

## 2026-11-16: engine speed refactor

Derrick picked the engine refactor. Structure-keyed caches (`Game.restruct`), indexes for triggers, replacements,
rule statics and add sources, a cached board list, faster clones (plain-dict objects, shared immutable Mods) and
memoized payment checks inside `legal_actions`. Every change was checked with a determinism hash: the same games
are played move for move (gen 14, greedy, random, expert and a full search-v2 game), the 370 tests pass with
`RIFTSHARK_CACHE_CHECK=1`, and a 40-game rules audit plus 60 smoke games report nothing.

| AI | before | after | speed-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| gen 14 (3 games) | 50.3 s | 36-37 s | 1.35x |
| expert (6 games) | 25.0 s | 16.9 s | 1.48x |
| search-v2 (1 game) | 936 s | 660 s | 1.42x |

The profile is now flat (no function above ~4% self time): the rest is spread over legal actions, cleanups,
clones and payments. A bigger jump needs a faster interpreter (PyPy runs this pure-Python engine unchanged, typically
3-5x) or a rewrite of the hot paths in a compiled language.

## 2026-11-17: step 3 again, with 1000 search-v2 games

- 700 more search-v2 self-play games (`out/pol_sv2c.npz`, seed 9100000, 76,059 decisions, ~11 h of 4-core time spread
  over a day with four container restarts; the run resumed from its checkpoint each time). Together with the 300 from
  2026-11-14: 1000 games, 108,595 decisions. Data in /mnt/project-files/riftshark/learn.
- 69 of the new games were held out (`pol_sv2c_va`). How well each policy net predicts search-v2's pick there:

| policy net | trained on | val loss | top-1 agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| policy_g14 (current) | gen-14 games | 1.433 | 53.9% |
| policy_sv2b | gen 14 + 300 search-v2 (x3) | 1.313 | 53.5% |
| policy_sv2d (gen 14 + 1000 sv2 x3) | | 1.242 | 53.8% |
| policy_sv2d_ft (policy_g14 fine-tuned on sv2) | | 1.123 | 54.5% |
| **policy_sv2d_only** (1000 sv2 games only) | | **1.113** | **54.8%** |

  `tools/train_policy.py` now keeps the epoch with the lowest validation loss (the search-v2-only net peaks at epoch 2
  and then overfits), and has `--init` (fine-tune) and `--eval` (score a model on held-out files).
- Gate: search-v2 with `policy_sv2d_only` in place of `policy_g14` (its pruning and opponent model) vs search-v2,
  100 mirror games, seed 95000: **51-49 (51%, interval 41-61%)**. It led 11-3 after 14 games, which was noise.
  `train_loop.py gate --log FILE` now writes one line per game and resumes after a restart.

Conclusion: a policy net that predicts search-v2 better (loss 1.43 -> 1.11) does not make search-v2 stronger. The
policy net only picks which 3 options to search and how the opponent plays; both were already good enough. The
strength is set by the judge (gen 14's one-turn value) and the number of samples. More distillation data on this
machine (~1000 games per 2 days) will not move it; the levers are a better judge or much more compute.
