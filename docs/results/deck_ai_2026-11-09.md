# Which AI plays Kennen and Riven best (2026-11-09)

One deck plays all 10 other tournament decks (`tools/deck_vs.py`). Seats alternate, and each pair of games shares a
shuffle. The opponents always use plain gen 8 (`learned:gen_008`). Only the AI playing the tested deck changes.

## First run (10 games per matchup, seed 710000)

| AI playing the deck | Kennen | Riven |
|---|---|---|
| `learned:gen_008` (plain gen 8) | 40/100 (40%) | 47/99 (47.5%) |
| `learned-rl:gen_008` (learned AI answers for the opponent in the lookahead) | 30/94 (32%) | 39/94 (41.5%) |
| `learned-deep:gen_008:0.15:3` (close calls re-checked through the opponent's whole turn) | 35/94 (37%) | 54/94 (57.5%) |

## Riven confirmation (20 games per matchup, fresh seed 720000)

| AI playing Riven | score | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|
| plain gen 8 | 90/200 (45.0%) | 38-52% |
| deep | 110/188 (58.5%) | 51-65% |

12 deep games hit the 300 s game limit and were dropped (6 of them against Azir). Counting all 12 as losses still
gives 55%. Over both runs, deep Riven scores 164/282 (58%) and plain Riven 137/299 (46%).

Per matchup with deep (fresh seeds): Kennen 16/19 (plain 8/20), Akali 16/20 (12), Irelia 16/20 (15), Nasus 13/19 (8),
Kai'Sa 9/19 (3), Rek'Sai 7/19 (4), Viktor 6/18 (7), Master Yi 3/20 (7).

## Reading

- Re-checking close calls through the opponent's turn makes Riven about 12 points stronger. Riven holds counters and
  tricks, and the one-turn lookahead cannot see their value. The same search ties gen 8 in mirror gates (51.6%) and
  does not help Kennen. A gain for one deck type disappears in an average over all decks.
- Kennen is not fixed by search. Its losses come from what it plays (gear to an empty base, Equipment moved back and
  forth, no contesting), not from missing the opponent's answers.
- A simulated opponent answering during our turn (`learned-rl`) hurts both decks.

## The judge without card identities (2026-11-10)

Gen 8 learned that some cards are good just for sitting on our board. In one game, the hashed card-identity feature
for Edge of Night on our board added +0.6 logit, which outweighed every other term. So the AI dumped the gear into an
empty base on turn 2 instead of playing Trifarian Captain. Most likely the gear sits on the board in games Kennen wins
because it gets attached late.

`train --linear --mask 106-233` trains the same linear judge on gens 6-8 without the card identities of our hand and
our board (`models/cand_mhb.json`). The counts of units, gear, might and so on stay.

| test | gen 8 | masked judge |
|---|---|---|
| mirror gate vs gen 8 (seeds 74000, 75000) | - | 108/200, 104/200 (53% over 400) |
| Kennen vs all decks, 20 per matchup (seed 790000) | 100/200 (50%) | 114/200 (57%) |
| Kennen vs all decks, 10 per matchup (seed 710000) | 40/100 | 46/99 |
| Riven vs all decks, 20 per matchup (seed 720000) | 90/200 (45%) | 114/200 (57%) |
| Riven + deep search, same seeds | 110/188 (58.5%) | 95/176 (54%) |

Variants: masking only our board's card identities scored 45.5% in the mirror gate, and Kennen 34%. The masked judge on
2.5x the data scored 54.3%, then 48% on fresh seeds. Masking every card identity scored 50.3%.

Reading: in mirrors the masked judge plays even with gen 8, but it plays the two best decks in the real format clearly
better: Kennen +7 points over 300 games, Riven +12 over 200. With the masked judge, deep search adds nothing more for
Riven.
